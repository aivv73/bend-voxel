# Store sparse voxel bodies as spatial trees of solid cuboids

The demolition district must contain millions of individually editable 10 cm cells without allocating the surrounding air. We use disjoint, half-open cuboids of uniform material in a balanced bounding-volume tree, owned by each voxel body. Bounds are signed integral cell coordinates represented by exact F32 integers within ±2²³ cells. This replaces the 40 × 24 × 20 ownership array. World content dimensions and the number of moving bodies are independent configuration choices.

We considered the proposed 32³ chunks. Cuboids compress the district's large slabs and thin beams directly, keep the implementation in Bend, and allow connectivity to traverse thousands of solid regions instead of millions of cells. Sphere removal subdivides touched leaves down to individual cells; connectivity follows positive-area shared faces across all regions of an affected body. Meshing subtracts neighboring face coverage, including partial coverage and material boundaries. Region boundaries have no gameplay meaning. The spatial tree also serves picking. Initial assemblies must be disjoint and six-connected; generation tests verify connectivity.

The trade-off is workload sensitivity: repeated irregular destruction can produce many small regions. This representation does not establish an arbitrary-world scalability limit. Record region counts, surface counts, editing phases, and real occupied-cell counts when comparing workloads. Streaming, an index over all bodies, region compaction after extensive damage, and hierarchical component summaries remain future options if measurements justify them.

A body keeps its local geometry identity while moving. Carving creates new IDs for affected connected components; unrelated bodies retain theirs. Bend faces, native transport records, and Vulkan mesh slots survive camera changes, aim changes, and translation. Only edited mesh slots are uploaded; view-dependent previews are separate. IDs cannot be reused for different live geometry. The revision field permits future in-place identity-preserving geometry updates.

Material explicitly marks protected anchors. A configurable detached-body budget rejects an entire edit when exceeded, preserving cells, ownership, and motion. The first district retains vertical gravity and ground stopping; it does not add rotation, body collisions, stacking, or reattachment.
