# Real-Time Meshlet Extraction from Scalar Volumes: applicability to the voxel engine

Date: 2026-09-23. Primary sources: [Kreskowski, Rendle, and Froehlich, paper preprint](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf), [supplement](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Supplementary-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf), [authors' code and README at `b5f81a6`](https://github.com/Nyran/Real-Time-Meshlet-Extraction-from-Scalar-Volumes/tree/b5f81a61e6fbb718cfe437f4b72d8971e178d8e8). The [university publication page](https://www.uni-weimar.de/en/media/chairs/computer-science-department/vr/research/real-time-rendering/real-time-meshlet-extraction-from-scalar-volumes/?type=98) links these materials. Section and figure references below refer to the paper.

## What the method does

The input is a sampled **scalar volume** and an isovalue. The output is a persistent, indexed triangle isosurface, using Marching Cubes cases and the Parallel Marching Blocks (PMB) scheme to share vertices inside each block. A per-block min/max volume supports GPU filtering of blocks whose scalar range contains the isovalue. A task shader analyzes each occupied 8 × 8 × 4-cell block, counts the surface vertices and triangles, and partitions it hierarchically into one 8 × 8 × 4, up to four 4 × 4 × 4, or 2 × 2 × 2 subblocks. It sends compact occupied-cell indices through the task/mesh shader payload. One mesh shader workgroup per chosen subblock writes positions, normals, 8-bit triangle indices, a descriptor, and optionally culling bounds into persistent global buffers. Another task/mesh pipeline can cull and draw these meshlets. The evaluation targets at most **128 vertices and 254 triangles per meshlet**, with at most 128 occupied cells processed by one extraction workgroup. [Paper §§3.1–3.3, Figures 2 and 4](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf); [supplement, Listings 1–2](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Supplementary-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf); [authors' pipeline guide](https://github.com/Nyran/Real-Time-Meshlet-Extraction-from-Scalar-Volumes/blob/b5f81a61e6fbb718cfe437f4b72d8971e178d8e8/README.md).

The key gain over a small fixed block is to keep meshlets large enough for efficient drawing while still meeting output limits. The occupancy list also avoids running extraction threads on empty cells. The task and mesh stages exchange this list through on-chip memory. A 2 × 2 × 2 fallback always fits their selected limits, although block-level partitioning cannot pack triangles as tightly as an offline meshlet optimizer. [Paper §§3.2.1–3.2.2 and §5](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf).

## What the measurements establish

All reported timings were on an **RTX 4090 with 24 GB VRAM**, i9-13900K, at **3840 × 2160**, with ten measurements averaged. Test scalar volumes ranged from 256³ / 16 MB to roughly 1024² × 1546 / 3 GB. The online baseline is the authors' **compute-shader PMB reimplementation**, choosing the fastest block size for each dataset, rather than a conventional voxel-face or greedy mesher. An offline reference uses meshoptimizer to form optimized meshlets; its creation time is excluded from the frame-time comparison. [Paper §4 and Table 1](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf).

|---|---:|---|
| Extraction time | 20.8% less on average; 14.0% to 36.1% less by dataset | Versus fastest PMB block mode for each dataset; includes occupied-block filtering. [Paper §4.2, Figure 9](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf) |
| Extraction plus draw | 20.3% less on average; 11.6% to 27.4% less | Versus fastest PMB mode across the tested isovalue ranges, with the isovalue changed each frame. The Figure 1 Chameleon example reports **1.7 ms extraction plus rendering** for this method against **3.1 ms** for PMB, but that example and all reported performance numbers are RTX 4090 results, not predictions for the GTX 1660. The method produces two to three times as many meshlets as the offline reference because it cannot precisely pack arbitrary triangle groups. Turbulent data makes more small meshlets and narrows the speed advantage. The authors found normal-cone construction too costly for their extraction path and favor implicit or explicit AABBs. [Paper Figures 1 and 8–10, §§3.2.2, 4.3, 5](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf).

## Lifetime and changing voxels

The paper's interactive workload changes the **isovalue of a scalar volume**, programmatically at each frame. Figure 2 labels filtering and extraction as running once per isovalue change and rendering at least once per frame. Thus, with an unchanged volume and isovalue, the extracted meshlets can be reused for camera changes and later passes; the paper's main speedups arise when they **must be regenerated frequently**. The paper describes PMB's min/max volume as offline preprocessing and does not benchmark edits to scalar samples or local dirty-region updates. If voxel values change, the corresponding min/max entries, occupancy decisions, geometry, and bounds must be made current before reuse; a partial-update scheme and its synchronization costs would be additional engine work. [Paper introduction, §§3.1, 4.3, Figure 2](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf). This last update requirement is an inference from the pipeline's data dependencies, not a measured result of the paper.

## Hardware, API, and code fit

The published implementation is **C++20 + OpenGL 4.6 + `GL_NV_mesh_shader`**, with compute shaders for occupied-block preparation and task/mesh shaders for extraction and drawing. Its README requires a discrete NVIDIA Turing-or-newer GPU and recent driver; the authors tested an RTX 4090 with driver 572.60 on Linux and Windows. The paper also reports a Vulkan prototype with similar performance on Chameleon, but the released framework and evaluation focus on OpenGL; its authors say a vendor-independent OpenGL port was possible but was not their measured implementation. [Paper §4 implementation details](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf); [authors' README](https://github.com/Nyran/Real-Time-Meshlet-Extraction-from-Scalar-Volumes/blob/b5f81a61e6fbb718cfe437f4b72d8971e178d8e8/README.md).

**Local capability check (2026-09-23):** `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=name,driver_version,compute_cap --format=csv,noheader` returned `NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1660, 610.57.04, 7.5`; `glxinfo -l` reported `OpenGL version string: 4.6.0 NVIDIA 610.57.04`, the GTX 1660 renderer, and `GL_NV_mesh_shader`. The required OpenGL extension is therefore exposed on this machine; no claim about its performance or successful execution of the authors' full framework follows from this capability check. The present renderer is a Bend3D/CUDA path, and project architecture treats an external graphics backend as a separate integration experiment. [Local architecture](../architecture.md), [authors' README](https://github.com/Nyran/Real-Time-Meshlet-Extraction-from-Scalar-Volumes/blob/b5f81a61e6fbb718cfe437f4b72d8971e178d8e8/README.md).

## Recommendation for this engine

**Do not implement this extraction method in the current renderer.** The implemented world is a 40 × 24 × 20 array of discrete owner IDs, with 4,640 occupied cells in the initial scene. It caches exposed, merged face rectangles per body after an accepted cut; camera motion and the current rigid vertical body motion reuse those surfaces. Neighboring cells with different owners deliberately expose a face even when both cells are occupied. A single binary scalar field would lose that boundary and Marching Cubes would change sharp voxel silhouettes, face normals, and picking behavior. Preserving body and material boundaries would need additional fields or a separate extraction per owner. [World representation and exposure rule](../../src/world.bend), [rectangle rendering and Bend3D handoff](../../src/render.bend).

The observed costs also do not establish this paper's extraction stage as the bottleneck. In the forced-CUDA stage probe, mean per-frame scene preparation was 6.7–7.3 ms, rendering 15.1–16.5 ms, and render-cell disposal 7.2–7.3 ms. An accepted edit in run 1 averaged roughly 28 ms carving and 37 ms **combined** connectivity and meshing; the latter does not isolate surface generation. The controlled two-round comparison recorded CPU frame p95 around 20–23 ms at 2–12 workers versus CUDA frame p95 around 43–46 ms, under the same scheduled workload. These wall-clock measurements do not attribute the gap to geometry processing. [Stage probe and boundaries](../profiling.md), [measured stages](../demo-validation.md), [controlled comparison](controlled-cpu-cuda.md).

The [split-stage benchmark](../demo-validation.md#surface-generation-timing-2026-09-23) measured roughly 19 ms median surface generation versus 15–16 ms median connectivity per accepted edit on the GTX 1660. This makes the current face builder worth examining, but it does not establish that the paper's scalar-field method would accelerate it. Also inspect scene preparation, the render boundary, and cell disposal with the existing workload. If a future engine uses dense smooth scalar fields or very large frequently regenerated surfaces, test the authors' released OpenGL pipeline as a separate backend on the GTX 1660. Compare complete edit-to-visible-frame latency, stable-frame cost, memory use, and visual/ownership correctness against the current renderer. The paper's RTX 4090 speedup relative to PMB is not an estimate of that comparison. [Paper §§4–5](https://www.uni-weimar.de/fileadmin/user/fak/medien/professuren/Virtual_Reality/documents/publications/2026-Real-Time_Meshlet_Extraction__preprint.pdf), [authors' code](https://github.com/Nyran/Real-Time-Meshlet-Extraction-from-Scalar-Volumes/tree/b5f81a61e6fbb718cfe437f4b72d8971e178d8e8).
